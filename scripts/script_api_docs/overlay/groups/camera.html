The camera the picture comes from, and the camera rig that moves it. No function here takes an entity: they all act on the active camera, the one marked Main, or the first camera in the scene when none is. The rig functions act on that camera's Camera Rig component; without one they do nothing and read 0.

  • Position and rotation are the camera's own transform. getPosition and getRotation read it and the setters write it, relative to the camera's parent. A camera parented under an entity at (10, 0, 0) and sitting 5 m behind it reads 0, 2, 5, not 10, 2, 5. For a camera at the top of the hierarchy that is the world position. While a rig drives the camera it rewrites the transform every frame, so setPosition/setRotation last until the next frame; setFov stays.
  • Rig numbers are forgiving, not checked. setRigMode treats anything but 0 as third person. setTargetYawMode treats anything but 0 and 2 as Follow. addYawPitch clamps the pitch to the rig's limits (by default −80° to 75°) but lets the yaw run past 360. setLagSpeeds turns negative speeds into 0. setArmLength takes a negative length as it is, which puts the camera in front of the target.
  • Shake moves the transform, the FOV kick does not. The rig writes the shaken pose into the camera's transform, so getPosition and getRotation jitter while a shake runs. A FOV kick is added on top of the field of view and getFov keeps answering the authored value. playShake returns 0 when there is no rig; handles start at 1.
  • Blending. blendTo(camera, seconds, curve) makes that camera the main one at once (every other camera loses Main), so from the next call on the other functions act on it. The ease itself needs a Camera Rig on the camera blended to; without one it is a cut and isBlending stays false. Curves outside 0 to 2 become smoothstep.
